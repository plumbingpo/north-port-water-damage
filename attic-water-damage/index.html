Attic Water Damage: What North Port Homeowners Should Know

There are two ways an attic ends up wet. Rain gets in from above through worn shingles, failed flashing, or the seals around vents and chimneys. Moisture also rises from the house below and condenses on cold roof sheathing when the attic is not ventilated well. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. Our team in North Port inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. Even worse, it keeps water pressed against the framing and the ceiling drywall for weeks. Our process is to pull the saturated insulation, dry the wood using dehumidifiers and air movers, and treat mold found on rafters or sheathing.

Warning Signs

  • Nail points coming through the sheathing that are rusty or beaded with water
  • A patch of insulation that is flatter, more matted, or darker than the rest
  • A bath fan or ceiling light fixture with drips or a stain around it
  • After rain, a stain or brown ring on a top floor ceiling that looks worse
  • Dark stains or streaks on the rafters and the underside of the roof deck
  • Light from outside visible along a roof valley or around a chimney or vent pipe
The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold May Begin

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

Weeks later

Stains Come Through

After repeated wetting, the insulation is saturated and water reaches the ceiling. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

After months

Wood Starts Rotting

The sheathing comes apart in layers and no longer grips the roofing nails. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Insulation That Stops Working

Compressed, damp insulation does very little insulating, and it may not recover after it dries. Rooms under the wet section run hotter or colder, and energy bills rise.

Wet Wiring

Attics hold junction boxes, recessed light housings and cable runs. Do not enter the attic before someone checks the power, because wet electrical parts can shock you or start a fire.

Air Conditioner Drips

In a lot of homes the attic houses an air handler along with its condensate line and pan. For weeks an overflowing pan or blocked drain can drip into insulation, and there is no storm to tip you off.

Bath Fans Venting Inside

Every day, a fan duct that stops short of the roof releases humid bathroom air into the attic. That moisture condenses on the cold sheathing and looks just like a roof leak.

Saturated or moldy insulation is bagged and removed so the wood and drywall under it can dry. The access path is kept contained and the living area underneath is protected.

With dehumidifiers and air movers, the sheathing, joists and rafters return to normal moisture levels. We clean visible mold off the wood, then apply an antimicrobial.

4

Fix the Cause

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. Once the attic stays dry, new insulation goes in to restore the R-value.
